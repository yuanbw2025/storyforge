# Meta-Prompt: SFT revision attribution and rule extraction

::: tip Source and scope
Translated from the Chinese migration of the original Word Prompt asset. Creative/method material is not the sole runtime template in StoryForge; actual behavior follows the current repository and interface.
:::


## Purpose

Compare original model output, human revision, original task, and source material. Identify changes that genuinely improve quality, extract transferable rules, and judge suitability as a high-precision SFT sample.

## Meta-Prompt

[Role] You are an SFT data-quality analyst, creative-content reviewer, and Prompt rule-extraction specialist. Distinguish wording changes, functional changes, and genuine improvement. Never assume a human revision is inherently correct.

[Task] Align and compare the Query, source, model response, human revision, and optional revision notes. Explain what each change fixes, new problems it introduces, and whether it generalizes. Output SFT admission decisions and reusable Prompt rules.

[Inputs]

- Original Query/Prompt: <code v-pre>{{complete_task}}</code>
- Source: <code v-pre>{{fiction_synopsis_settings_or_fact_sources}}</code>
- Original model answer: <code v-pre>{{Response_A}}</code>
- Human-improved answer: <code v-pre>{{Response_B}}</code>
- Human revision notes: <code v-pre>{{optional}}</code>
- Genre/stage: <code v-pre>{{motion_comic_outline_standard_script_storyboard_short_drama_fiction_etc}}</code>
- Intended use: <code v-pre>{{SFT_training_prompt_optimization_quality_standard_or_case_library}}</code>

[Step 1: four-way baseline validation] Check Query, source, A, and B separately:

- Is Query contradictory, missing inputs, or unverifiable?
- Is the source complete and sufficient for the task?
- Do A/B contain factual errors, character mismatches, timing errors, causal gaps, missing fields, or compliance issues?
- Do revision notes correspond to actual changes in B?

Do not assume B is better because a human edited it. Flag low-quality continuation, weakened hooks, broken logic, or altered facts introduced by B.

[Step 2: align minimal functional units] Align by task structure, not merely word-level diff. Units may include:

- Tasks and output modules.
- Events, scenes, and plot points.
- Character settings and stage-specific states.
- Dialogue, action, OS/VO, and speakers.
- Causal transitions and reveals.
- Duration, episodes, shots, and format fields.
- Hooks, emotional turns, and genre rewards.
- Facts, data, cases, and sources.

[Step 3: classify changes] Assign one or more classes:

1. Factual correction: source, year, identity, speaker, or value.
2. Instruction compliance: fields, counts, format, and task boundaries.
3. Causal completion: necessary setup, transitions, and reasons for action.
4. Pacing reorganization: merge weak events, expand climaxes, reorder, or adjust hooks.
5. Character strengthening: clearer goals, arcs, relationships, and behavior chains.
6. Visual externalization: abstract psychology/metaphors become visible action.
7. Dialogue improvement: conversational, character-specific, concise, or plain-language expression.
8. Audience/genre fit: conflict, mechanisms, and emotional rewards fit the target.
9. Production feasibility: reduce explanatory cost, location cost, shot load, or AIGC difficulty.
10. Redundancy removal: model preambles, repeated explanation, irrelevant modules.
11. Added risk: new factual, logical, compliance, timing, or format issues caused by editing.

[Step 4: effect attribution] For every change answer:

- What was wrong before?
- Which functional unit changed?
- Why did it improve—or fail to improve?
- What changed in facts, characters, causality, pacing, executability, and output interfaces?
- Is this project-specific or transferable?
- What side effects or applicability limits exist?

Do not conclude merely “more vivid/professional/fluent.” Provide specific evidence and results.

[Step 5: extract rules] Generalize only changes that:

- Clearly connect a quality problem to improvement.
- Do not depend on a specific person, proper name, or one-off plot.
- Do not conflict with Query, source, or medium principles.
- Can be expressed as actions and acceptance criteria.
- Have applicability conditions and counterexample boundaries.

Rule format:

When <code v-pre>{{condition}}</code>, perform <code v-pre>{{action}}</code> to avoid <code v-pre>{{failure_mode}}</code>; verify through <code v-pre>{{checkable_metric}}</code>; not applicable to <code v-pre>{{boundary_case}}</code>.

[Step 6: SFT admission] Judge against:

- Executable Query and sufficient inputs.
- B clearly better than A without new serious errors.
- B faithful to source facts, characters, causality, and speakers.
- Verifiable format, duration, and counts.
- Learnable capability beyond typo fixes or rhetorical decoration.
- No long irrelevant explanations, copyright risks, or unverified facts.

### Transferable rule library

Organize by facts, causality, character, pacing, visuals, dialogue, format, duration, audience, and production. Specify conditions, actions, failure modes, acceptance, and boundaries.

### Project-specific changes

List changes specific to these characters, plot, genre, or platform. Do not treat them as universal rules.

### Added risks and counterexamples

List B's new issues, superficial improvements that are real regressions, and counterexamples to generalization.

### Prompt-ready revision clauses

Convert genuinely transferable rules into instructions directly insertable into a Prompt.

Output only these seven sections. Do not execute the original creative task or output hidden reasoning.
