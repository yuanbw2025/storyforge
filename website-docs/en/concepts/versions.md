# Sealing, production, release, and saves

## How the three stages connect

| Stage | Your action | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 World sealing | Confirm world content and scope | A definite, referenceable world version |
| S2 Product direction | Configure goals, sources, and rules inside TTRPG/chat/town/etc. | An explicit production plan |
| S3 Product execution | Explicitly start production, inspect outputs, release, and run | Product versions and independent saves |

Some products allow an S2 draft without world sources. Formal production still requires source and product checks; saving a draft is not formally starting.

Independent longform, short fiction, and novel adaptations do not require world sealing first; they have their own creation workflows.

## Does publishing put it online?

Product publishing usually preserves an immutable local version for export or play. It does not automatically upload to a public website, create a community account, or launch a commercial service.

Check attribution, licensing, and asset rights separately when sharing files.

## Why old versions do not change

A frozen version preserves its content at the time; later revisions form new versions. Old exports, games, and saves remain traceable to their original evidence.

World versions, product versions, and saves are three different objects. A world upgrade does not automatically upgrade a product; a new product version must not be assumed to overwrite existing saves.

## Author checks

Before release, check source versions, candidate adoption, serious issues, and asset completeness. After release, check which version export or play uses, then retain a [full backup](/en/guides/backup-restore).
